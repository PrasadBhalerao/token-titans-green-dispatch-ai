## Overview

"Prompt, Plan, Preserve: Hacking Sustainability with Agentic AI" is a hackathon focused on using agentic AI for measurable sustainability impact. The event asks teams to build agents that reduce emissions, waste, and manual effort, while also justifying the agent's own compute footprint through efficient, traceable, and responsible design.

## Problem Worth Solving

Organizations often have sustainability goals, but the operational work needed to turn those goals into outcomes is slow and manual. Sustainability-related data may be spread across invoices, portals, spreadsheets, sensors, and billing exports. The challenge is to create agentic AI workflows that can help turn this fragmented data into decisions and measurable impact without creating unnecessary digital waste.

Teams are expected to:

- Identify a concrete sustainability problem, including who is affected, how often it happens, and what the current pain looks like.
- Build an agentic workflow with planning, tool use, orchestration, failure handling, and human involvement at important decision points.
- Measure what changed using sustainability-relevant units such as emissions avoided, energy saved, water saved, carbon intensity reduced, waste diverted, asset performance improved, hours removed, or data quality improved.
- State the baseline and assumptions behind the measured impact.
- Deliver the result where the decision is made, instead of only producing a report afterwards.
- Prove that the agent earns its keep by reporting model selection, token efficiency, tool discipline, retry control, carbon-aware design, observability, privacy, auditability, and sustainability outcome.

## Goal

Deliver a working agentic solution that helps organizations understand, track, and reduce sustainability impact, or make a better sustainability decision now, with an auditable trail and a compute footprint justified by the value created.

## What To Submit

Teams need to submit the following artifacts:

- Proof of concept: Repository link with a README covering setup, run instructions, sample data, and known limitations so judges can run it without follow-up questions. No customer code, confidential or client data, or client branding.
- Agent design document: Include an architecture diagram covering goal, user, data sources, tools, orchestration, decision points, human oversight, and failure handling.
- Pitch deck: 8 to 10 slides covering the problem, solution, how the agent works, methodology, results, impact, efficiency choices, and roadmap.
- Demo video: 3 to 5 minutes of the agent running. It should not be just a slide walkthrough. The video is used for pre-screening, and a missing video can prevent shortlisting.

## Rules

- Teams may have up to 5 members.
- Mixed teams across geography, roles, and gender are encouraged and credited.
- Teams should select a challenge theme and clearly state the sustainability pain point their agentic AI solution addresses.
- Suggested tracks include carbon and ESG intelligence; reporting, disclosure, and assurance; procurement and circularity; and forecasting, scheduling, and dispatch.
- The solution must be agentic, showing planning, tool use, orchestration, traceability, error handling, and a feedback loop.
- If a single prompt can solve the problem, the use case is not challenging enough.
- Human oversight is mandatory. Agents may recommend and draft, but humans must approve actions with financial, contractual, legal, or reputational consequences.
- Use only public, open, or synthetic data.
- Customer, confidential, and personal data are not permitted.
- If realistic data is required, generate it and clearly state that it is synthetic.
- Agent sustainability figures are required and are part of the evaluation.
- Submissions without agent sustainability figures cannot score in the agent sustainability criterion.
- Submissions may reference recognized standards such as Software Carbon Intensity (SCI) Specification / ISO/IEC 21031:2024, GHG Protocol Corporate and Scope 3 Standards, ESRS, and ISSB.
- All work must be completed and pitched within the hackathon timeline.
- Submissions must be original.
- Any pre-existing or open-source components must be declared in the README.
- Plagiarism will result in disqualification.
- Final decisions rest with the jury.

## Judging Criteria

- Sustainability Impact & Measurability: Measurable sustainability outcomes backed by a clear baseline, assumptions, and evidence.
- Agentic Depth & Technical Implementation: Planning, tool use, orchestration, traceability, error handling, feedback, and maintainable implementation.
- Sustainability of the Agent Itself: Efficient model and tool use, carbon awareness, observability, privacy, auditability, and guardrails.
- Demo & Communication: Clear problem definition, working solution demo, methodology explanation, and ability to defend the results.
- Innovation & Creativity: Fresh thinking, unique approaches, or bold applications of agentic AI to sustainability challenges.

Note: Final evaluation and all decisions are at the discretion of the jury.
